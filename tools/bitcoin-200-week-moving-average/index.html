Is bitcoin cheap right now?

Today’s price against bitcoin’s 200-week moving average, with the whole history on one chart.

Bitcoin’s 200-week moving average is the average of its last 200 weekly prices, roughly 3.8 years. It is a slow line that shows the long-run trend. When the price sits near or below it, that has been historically rare and has lined up with past bear-market lows. When the price runs far above it, the market has been stretched. This tracker shows where bitcoin stands today and how often history looked like this. It describes the past; it does not predict the future.

How it works. The saved file holds one price per Sunday (00:00 UTC) from 2010-08-22. A free GitHub Action rebuilds it every week from the Blockchain.com source. The 200-week average is the plain average of the latest 200 weekly prices, and the current, still-forming week counts as the newest one. The multiple is price divided by that average. The first 200-week average needs 200 weeks of history, so the lines and zones start in mid-2014. When you open the page it tries to read a live price from Coinbase Exchange, a single exchange, so it can differ slightly from the exchange-average history. If that request fails, the page uses the newest daily price in the saved file. “How often this cheap” is the share of weeks since the first 200-week average with a multiple at or below today’s. “What came next” compares each week’s price with the price 52 weeks later, using only weeks that have a full year after them.

Common questions

What is the bitcoin 200-week moving average?

It is the plain average of bitcoin’s last 200 weekly prices, about 3.8 years of data. Because it moves slowly, it shows bitcoin’s long-run trend and smooths out the big rallies and crashes. This tracker divides today’s price by that average to give a multiple: 1.0x means price equals the average, 2.0x means price is double it.

Is bitcoin a buy when it falls below the 200-week moving average?

Not automatically. Historically, bitcoin has spent only a small share of weeks near or below this line, and those stretches have sat close to bear-market lows. But that is a pattern from a short history with a handful of separate episodes, and bitcoin has often kept falling or stayed low for months after touching it. Treat it as one piece of context for a plan you can stick to, not as a signal. This is education, not financial advice.

How are the colored zones decided?

The zones are rounded cut-offs on the price-to-average multiple: Deep value below 1.1x, Fair from 1.1x to 2x, Stretched from 2x to 3.5x and Overheated above 3.5x. They are a way to read the chart, not a forecast, and different people would draw the lines in different places.

Why is the chart on a log scale?

Bitcoin has gone from cents to tens of thousands of dollars. On a normal scale the early years would look flat. A log scale gives each doubling the same height, so a move from $1 to $2 looks the same as $30,000 to $60,000, and the zone bands keep a constant width.

Where does the data come from, and how current is it?

Weekly prices are the daily average USD price across major exchanges from the Blockchain.com Charts API, taken each Sunday, starting 2010-08-22. A free scheduled GitHub Action refreshes the saved file every week. When you open the page it also tries to fetch a live price from Coinbase Exchange’s public ticker. If that is blocked, the page uses the latest daily price from the saved file.

Does this tool give financial advice?

No. It describes where bitcoin’s price sits relative to its own history. It cannot tell you what bitcoin will do next, and past patterns may not repeat. Do not invest money you cannot afford to lose, and talk to a licensed professional before you make decisions.
