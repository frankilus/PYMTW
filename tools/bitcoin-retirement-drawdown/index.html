Sources and assumptions

2026 federal figures come straight from the IRS. Later years raise the brackets, deductions and 0%/15% thresholds with your inflation assumption; the net investment income tax thresholds stay fixed because the law does not index them.

  1. IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-32 (PDF): 2026 tax rate tables (§4.01), 0%/15% capital gains thresholds (§4.03), standard deduction and the extra amount for age 65+ (§4.14) ↗
  2. IRS: tax inflation adjustments for tax year 2026, including One Big Beautiful Bill amendments ↗
  3. IRS: Net Investment Income Tax (3.8%; $250,000 joint, $200,000 single or head of household) ↗
  4. IRS: Retirement plan and IRA required minimum distributions FAQs (RMDs start at age 73; Roth accounts have no lifetime RMDs) ↗
  5. IRS Publication 590-B, Appendix B, Table III (Uniform Lifetime Table) ↗
  6. Treasury and IRS final RMD regulations, 89 FR 58886 (applicable age 75 if born on or after January 1, 1960) ↗
  7. IRS: Digital assets (bitcoin is property for federal tax purposes) ↗
  8. IRS Topic 409: Capital gains and losses (long-term means held more than one year) ↗
  9. IRS Topic 423: Social Security benefits (part may be taxable) ↗

How the model works. Each year starts with withdrawals at that year’s bitcoin price, then balances grow. Spending and other income rise with inflation. RMDs use your prior year-end 401(k)/IRA balance and the Uniform Lifetime Table. Other income is treated as fully taxable, which overstates tax on Social Security. Bitcoin sales use your average cost basis, are treated as long-term, and are grossed up so the tax is paid from the sale. Extra cash, for example from an RMD bigger than you need, is held for the next year without growth. Scenarios change bitcoin only; your 401(k)/IRA and Roth earn your stated return in every scenario. The 75% crash happens the year you retire and the price recovers to the base-case path over four years. The bad-years scenarios use three years of −40%, −30% and −10% with the other years adjusted so the long-run average matches your growth rate. Low growth means bitcoin only keeps pace with inflation.

Common questions

How much bitcoin can I safely sell each year in retirement?

It depends on your stack, your other savings and income, taxes and when you retire. This planner finds the highest yearly spending, rising with inflation, that still lasts to the age you choose even if bitcoin falls 75% the year you retire or has its worst years first. It then shows the bitcoin you would sell each year to fund it.

How are bitcoin sales taxed in retirement?

The IRS treats bitcoin as property. Selling bitcoin you held for more than a year creates a long-term capital gain on the amount above your cost basis. That gain is taxed at 0%, 15% or 20%, stacked on top of your ordinary income such as 401(k) or IRA withdrawals, pensions and taxable Social Security. A 3.8% net investment income tax can apply above $250,000 of modified AGI for joint filers or $200,000 for single and head-of-household filers.

What is the 0% capital gains bracket for 2026?

For 2026 the 0% rate applies to long-term gains while taxable income, including the gain, stays at or below $98,900 for married couples filing jointly, $66,200 for heads of household and $49,450 for single filers (IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32). Retirees with modest ordinary income can often sell some bitcoin each year with no federal tax on the gain.

Do required minimum distributions change how much bitcoin I should sell?

They can. RMDs from a 401(k) or traditional IRA start at age 73, or 75 if you were born in 1960 or later. Each RMD is your prior year-end balance divided by the IRS Uniform Lifetime Table factor. RMDs are ordinary income, so they use up room in the 0% capital gains bracket and can make bitcoin sales more expensive. Roth accounts have no RMDs while you are alive.

Why test a 75% crash and bad years first?

Bitcoin has had deep drawdowns. If a crash hits early in retirement, you sell more coins at low prices to cover the same spending, and those coins are not there for the recovery. This is sequence-of-returns risk. The planner compares the same long-run growth with the bad years first and with the bad years last so you can see the difference.

Is this tax advice, and does it include state tax?

No. This is an educational estimate of federal income tax only, using 2026 IRS figures that are raised with your inflation assumption in later years. It leaves out state and local tax, the temporary senior deduction, the taxable share of Social Security, Medicare premium surcharges and capital loss rules. Check your plan with a tax professional before you sell.
