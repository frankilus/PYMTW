🚗 True-cost guide

The true cost of owning a car. It’s more than the payment.

Where the money really goes, a calculator for your own numbers, and the questions to ask before you sign. Every figure links to its source.

Owning and operating a new car costs an average of $12,863 a year, or $1,071.92 a month, according to AAA’s 2026 Your Driving Costs study, which assumes five years and 15,000 miles a year.1 The biggest piece is depreciation at $4,422 a year, followed by fuel, insurance, maintenance, finance charges, and taxes and fees. Your number depends on the price, your loan, how far you drive and where you live.

Per mile, the type of car and the price matter most

Most ownership costs are fixed, so the fewer miles you drive, the more each mile costs. AAA’s 2026 average for a new vehicle:2

$1.10per mile at 10,000 mi/yr
86¢per mile at 15,000 mi/yr
74¢per mile at 20,000 mi/yr
  • Vehicle type is a big lever. AAA found half-ton pickups cost $1.10 a mile, 48 cents more than small sedans, which adds an average of $7,191 a year.1
  • Real prices run higher than AAA’s sample. The average MSRP of the cars in AAA’s study is $39,376,2 but the average new vehicle actually sold for $50,562 in September 2026, before taxes and fees, according to Kelley Blue Book.4 A pricier car means more depreciation, interest and tax.
  • Used is cheaper up front. The average used-vehicle listing price was $27,235 in September 2026 (Cox Automotive/vAuto).5 Used loans usually carry higher rates, so compare the whole cost (step 3).
  • EV, hybrid or gas? AAA found EV charging cost about 66%–70% less than gas, but higher depreciation, fees and finance costs often outweighed the savings. Hybrids were the most consistently lower-cost alternative to gas in its comparison.1
3

What the averages leave out

National averages can’t see your street, your state or the dealer’s finance office. Budget separately for:

  • Parking and tolls. Not in AAA’s six categories. Add yours in the calculator under “More inputs.”
  • Sales tax at purchase. AAA spreads purchase taxes and fees across five years, but you pay them up front or finance them, and they depend on where you register the car.2
  • Dealer add-ons. GAP insurance, window etching, and extended warranties or service contracts are extras you buy and finance with the car. The FTC says it’s OK to say no, and it’s never OK for a dealer to charge you for add-ons without your consent.16
  • GAP insurance. It covers the difference between what you owe and what your insurer pays if the car is totaled or stolen. The CFPB says prices vary widely, your own insurer may sell it, and financing it adds interest.13
  • Rolled-in old debt. If you owe more on your trade-in than it’s worth, rolling that balance into the new loan makes the new loan more expensive.12
  • Repairs after the warranty. AAA’s maintenance figure is for a new car and includes a comprehensive extended warranty.2 Experian notes that long-term ownership can lead to higher repair costs.6

    Long loans: a lower payment, more interest, and a car worth less than you owe

    The CFPB says a longer loan can reduce your monthly payment, but you’ll pay more interest and risk negative equity (owing more than the car is worth) for longer.11 Long loans are now common:

    • A record 25.5% of financed new-vehicle purchases in Q3 2026 had terms of 84 months or longer. The average new-car buyer financed $44,664 at 7.0% APR and is on track to pay $9,938 in interest (Edmunds).7
    • In Q2 2026, 29.6% of trade-ins toward new vehicles had negative equity, averaging $6,884. Buyers who rolled it in averaged $944 a month and a projected $16,270 in interest (Edmunds).8
    • Before trading in, get your loan’s payoff amount (it can differ from the balance on your statement) and compare it with the car’s trade-in value.12 The calculator above compares what you’d still owe at year five with the car’s estimated value.

    The 20/4/10 rule: a common rule of thumb, not a law

    You will often see this guideline for keeping a car affordable. No regulator or lender requires it, and most buyers today fall outside it: the average new-car loan runs about 70 months.7 Treat it as a gut check, not a requirement.

    20%down, so you start with some equity.
    4 yearsor less on the loan.
    10%or less of gross income on monthly car costs. Some versions count only the payment; the calculator uses payment plus running costs.

    Buying vs. leasing: lower payment, different trade-offs

    With a lease you pay for the car’s depreciation over the lease term instead of its full value, which is why payments are usually lower. In Q2 2026, 23.75% of new vehicles were leased, and the average lease payment was $617 versus $765 for a new-car loan (Experian).6

    Buying

    • You own the car and build equity once it’s worth more than you owe.
    • No mileage limits. After payoff, no payment at all.
    • Older cars can need more repairs.

    Leasing

    • Lower monthly payment and a newer car every few years.
    • No equity, mileage limits and restrictions on modifications.
    • Read the fine print for end-of-lease charges.

    Source: Experian, Auto loan rates and financing (pros and cons of financing vs. leasing).6

    9

    Questions to ask at the dealer

    Your bargaining power is greatest before you’re on the lot.16 Bring these:

    1. “What’s the out-the-door price, in writing?” That is the total before financing, including taxes and fees, so you can compare dealers apples to apples.16
    2. “Can we settle price, trade-in and financing separately?” AAA advises keeping the vehicle price, finance rate and trade-in value as separate negotiations.1
    3. “Can you beat my preapproval?” Show the rate your bank or credit union offered.11
    4. “What’s the APR, the term and the total of all payments?” The CFPB defines total cost as principal, interest and any down payment or trade-in over the life of the loan. Compare that, not just the monthly payment.15
    5. “Which add-ons are in this contract, and are any required?” Extended warranties, GAP and credit insurance are generally optional. If you’re told one is required, ask where the contract says so. You can also cancel an optional add-on during the loan.14
    6. “Is there a prepayment penalty?” If there is, you can negotiate to have it removed.11
    7. “What incentives apply to this car?” Kelley Blue Book estimated the average incentive at $3,359 (6.6% of the average transaction price) in September 2026.4

    Common questions

    How much does it cost to own a car per year?

    AAA’s 2026 Your Driving Costs study puts the average cost to own and operate a new vehicle at $12,863 a year, or $1,071.92 a month, assuming five years of ownership and 15,000 miles a year. Your cost depends on the car, your loan, how far you drive and where you live.

    What is the biggest cost of owning a new car?

    Depreciation, the value the car loses. AAA estimates it at $4,422 a year on average for a new vehicle in 2026, more than insurance, fuel or finance charges.

    How much does it cost per mile to drive a car?

    AAA’s 2026 average for a new vehicle is about 86 cents a mile at 15,000 miles a year, $1.10 at 10,000 miles and 74 cents at 20,000 miles, because fixed costs like depreciation and insurance are spread over more miles. The IRS business mileage rate (76 cents a mile from July 1, 2026) is a tax deduction rate, not an estimate of your own costs.

    Is a longer car loan a bad idea?

    A longer loan lowers the monthly payment but usually means more total interest, and the CFPB notes it can leave you owing more than the car is worth for longer. Compare loans by total cost, not just the payment.

    Do I have to buy GAP insurance or an extended warranty to get a car loan?

    Generally no. The CFPB says extended warranties, GAP insurance and credit insurance are usually optional. If a dealer says one is required, ask where the contract says so.

    What is the 20/4/10 rule for buying a car?

    It is a common rule of thumb, not a law or lender requirement: put at least 20% down, finance for no more than four years, and keep monthly car costs under about 10% of your gross income.
