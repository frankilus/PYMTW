👶 True-cost guide

The true cost of having a baby. Plan for the first year and beyond.

Where the money goes in year one and after, a calculator for your own numbers, and the benefits to claim. Every figure links to its source.

Raising a child costs an average of $29,325 a year for the first five years with full-time infant day care, and $303,418 over 18 years, in LendingTree’s 2026 study.1 Pregnancy, birth and postpartum care add about $2,743 out of pocket on employer insurance.3 Your child care, location and benefits change the total.

After the day-care years

LendingTree estimates $12,061 annually for the next 13 years and $303,418 across 18 years, up 1.9% from $297,674. Its state totals range from $201,963 in New Hampshire to $412,661 in Hawaii.1 Brookings used a different model for a child born in 2015, estimating $310,605 with 4% inflation from 2021 onward.6 Neither estimate includes college. Leave, infant medical care and unexpected bills can add more.

    Before baby arrives

    Check the benefits available to you.

    5

    Add your baby to your health plan on time

    Birth triggers a Special Enrollment Period. Marketplace plans usually allow 60 days; job-based plans must allow at least 30.8 Ask which coverage tier applies: adding a baby does not always mean moving from single to family coverage. KFF’s 2025 average worker contribution for family coverage was $6,850 a year; it is not the extra cost of one child.9 Newborns in its claims study averaged $475 out of pocket in their first months, separate from the parent’s birth costs.3 Add your expected infant medical costs to the birth-cost input if you want them included.

    Parental leave: paid and protected are different

    FMLA gives eligible employees up to 12 weeks of unpaid, job-protected leave for a birth.10 BLS found 27% of civilian workers had access to paid family leave in March 2023; its definition changes for 2026, so that is a dated benchmark.11 Check state programs and your employer’s policy. In the calculator, enter only unpaid weeks and the take-home pay you would lose.

    7

    Tax breaks and benefits

    For tax year 2026, the Child Tax Credit maximum is $2,200 per qualifying child, with up to $1,700 refundable. Age, income, Social Security number and other rules apply; enter only the amount you expect to claim.12 A dependent care FSA can exclude up to $7,500 starting in 2026 if offered by your employer; your filing status and eligibility can lower the limit.13 Ask about the Child and Dependent Care Credit too; the same care expenses cannot support both benefits. The calculator does not model that credit.

    The Trump Account pilot offers a $1,000 deposit for eligible U.S.-citizen children born in 2025–2028 with a valid Social Security number, elected using Form 4547. It is long-term savings, not cash for current bills.14

    Common questions

    How much does it cost to have a baby in the first year?

    There is no single official number. Using LendingTree’s 2026 averages ($29,325 a year in the early years, including $17,264 for full-time infant day care), Peterson-KFF’s $2,743 average out-of-pocket cost for pregnancy, birth and postpartum care on an employer plan, and diapers at $80 to $100 a month, a first year with paid child care can run about $33,000. Without paid child care it is far lower.

    Source 1

    How much does it cost to raise a child to 18?

    LendingTree’s 2026 study puts it at $303,418, or $16,857 a year on average, for a two-earner married couple at the median family income. That is not adjusted for inflation and does not include college. Brookings estimated $310,605 for a middle-income child born in 2015, assuming 4% inflation.

    Source 1

    How much does it cost to give birth with insurance?

    For people with employer health plans, Peterson-KFF found pregnancy, birth and postpartum care averaged $20,416 in total, with $2,743 paid out of pocket (2021–2023 claims). A vaginal delivery averaged $15,712 ($2,563 out of pocket) and a C-section $28,998 ($3,071 out of pocket).

    Source 3

    When do I need to add my baby to my health insurance?

    Having a baby qualifies you for a Special Enrollment Period. On the Marketplace you usually have 60 days, and job-based plans must give you at least 30 days. Call HR or your insurer soon after the birth so you don’t miss the window.

    Source 8

    What tax breaks help with the cost of a baby?

    The Child Tax Credit is worth up to $2,200 per qualifying child, with up to $1,700 refundable, if you meet the IRS rules. Starting in 2026, a dependent care FSA can cover up to $7,500 of child care per household with pre-tax money if your employer offers one. The Child and Dependent Care Credit may also help.

    Source 12

    Is parental leave paid in the U.S.?

    FMLA itself does not require paid leave. It gives eligible employees up to 12 weeks of unpaid, job-protected leave for a birth. BLS found 27% of civilian workers had access to paid family leave in March 2023. Some states run their own paid leave programs, so check your state and your employer.

    Source 10

    What is the $1,000 Trump Account for babies?

    It is a federal pilot deposit of $1,000 into a new Trump Account for U.S.-citizen children with a valid Social Security number born from 2025 through 2028. Parents elect it on IRS Form 4547. It is long-term savings for the child, not money for today’s bills.

    Source 14
