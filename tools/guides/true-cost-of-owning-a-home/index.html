The true cost of owning a home. It’s more than the mortgage.

The bills that come with the keys, a calculator for your own home, and what to check before you buy. Every figure links to its source.

Property taxes and insurance

ATTOM reports a $4,427 average single-family tax bill for 2025, up 3%, with a 0.90% effective rate. State averages run from $1,081 in West Virginia to $10,499 in New Jersey.2 Bankrate’s $4,316 tax benchmark differs because it uses different data periods and adjustments.1

Bankrate’s July 2025 insurance rates averaged $2,470 for $300,000 of dwelling coverage, with examples from $834 in Vermont to $6,425 in Nebraska. Flood coverage is separate.3 Get an address-specific quote; the home’s sale price is not its insured rebuilding cost.

3

Maintenance: a range, not a prediction

Fannie Mae suggests saving 1%–4% of home value each year, with newer homes nearer the low end and homes over 30 years old nearer the high end.4 On the $417,500 September 2026 median existing-home price, that is $4,175–$16,700 a year.8 Keep a separate repair reserve and ask about the age of major systems.

    Before you buy

    The bills at closing and after.

    5

    Closing costs are separate from your down payment

    Freddie Mac suggests allowing 2%–5% of the price: $4,000–$10,000 on a $200,000 home. Earnest money is often 1%–2% and is normally credited toward closing, rather than added again. Lenders may collect escrow reserves too.5 LodeStar’s 2025 average of $4,528 covered title, settlement, recording and transfer charges; lender fees and prepaid items were additional.9 Use your Loan Estimate for a complete breakdown.12

    6

    PMI with a smaller down payment

    Freddie Mac gives a typical PMI range of $30–$70 a month per $100,000 borrowed. Conventional loans commonly require it below 20% down. You may request cancellation at 20% equity under the applicable rules; automatic termination generally occurs at the scheduled 78% balance if payments are current. FHA insurance differs.6 The calculator estimates the first year only; it does not predict a cancellation date.

    7

    HOA fees and special assessments

    In the 2024 Census data, 21.6 million of 86.6 million owner households paid a condo or HOA fee. The median among those paying was $135 a month; about 3 million paid more than $500.7 Ask for the budget, reserves and upcoming special assessments. Enter $0 only if there is no fee.

    8

    What your down payment could earn elsewhere

    Cash tied up in the purchase cannot earn a return elsewhere. For comparison, the calculator uses the 10-year Treasury yield of 5.10% on October 15, 2026, not a guaranteed future return.10 Freddie Mac’s 30-year fixed mortgage average was 7.40% that week.11 A home can gain or lose value; this short calculation does not decide whether buying beats renting.

    Buying

    Principal can build equity. A fixed-rate loan stabilizes principal and interest, but repairs, taxes and insurance can change.

    Renting

    You keep more flexibility and the landlord handles many repairs. Rent can rise.

    Common questions

    How much does it cost to own a home beyond the mortgage?

    Bankrate’s 2025 study put the average at $21,400 a year for a single-family home: $8,808 for maintenance, $4,494 for utilities, $4,316 for property taxes, $2,267 for insurance and $1,515 for internet and cable. It ranged from $12,579 in West Virginia to $34,573 in Hawaii.

    Source 1

    How much should I budget for home maintenance?

    A common rule of thumb is 1% to 4% of your home’s value each year, according to Fannie Mae. Newer homes can start near 1%. Homes more than 30 years old may need closer to 4%. On a $417,500 home, that is about $4,175 to $16,700 a year.

    Source 4

    What is the average property tax on a home?

    ATTOM found the average single-family home paid $4,427 in property taxes in 2025, an effective rate of 0.90% of its estimated value. Bills vary widely, from an average of $1,081 in West Virginia to $10,499 in New Jersey.

    Source 2

    How much are closing costs?

    Generally 2% to 5% of the purchase price, according to Freddie Mac, paid on top of your down payment. Compare the Loan Estimates from at least a few lenders, because fees vary.

    Source 5

    How much is PMI, and when does it go away?

    Freddie Mac says private mortgage insurance typically costs about $30 to $70 a month for every $100,000 you borrow. On a conventional loan it ends automatically when your balance is scheduled to reach 78% of the home’s original value, if you are current on payments, and you can ask to cancel it at 20% equity.

    Source 6

    How much are HOA fees?

    In 2024, about 21.6 million of the 86.6 million owner households paid a condo or HOA fee, and the median was $135 a month, according to the Census Bureau. About 3 million households paid more than $500 a month.

    Source 7

    What is the opportunity cost of a down payment?

    It is what that cash could have earned if you had not tied it up in the house. For comparison, the 10-year Treasury yielded 5.10% on October 15, 2026. That is not a prediction, and your home may also gain value, so weigh both with a rent vs. buy comparison.

    Source 10
