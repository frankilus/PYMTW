What does “vested” mean for my employer money?

Vested means yours to keep. Your employee contributions are fully vested. Employer money can follow different schedules. Optional: use one employer contribution source and its current vested percentage from your statement.

THE PLAIN-ENGLISH GUIDE

Start with the language.

Is my 401(k) an investment?

Your 401(k) is a workplace retirement account. The funds inside it are the investments. A contribution election tells payroll what to add; an investment election tells the plan where to put that money. Existing balances and new-contribution allocations can differ.

Traditional vs. Roth: what changes?

Traditional elective contributions generally reduce current federal taxable income; withdrawals are generally taxable. Roth contributions use after-tax income, and qualified withdrawals are tax-free. They share an employee contribution limit. Tax treatment and investment choice are separate decisions.

What is an expense ratio?

An ongoing annual fund cost expressed as a percentage of assets. At an unchanged $10,000 balance, a 0.50% ratio is about $50 a year. These expenses are generally reflected in fund returns, rather than appearing as a separate bill. Plan administration or service fees may be additional.

How does the employer match work?

A match depends on how much you contribute and your plan’s formula. “50% of the first 6%” means the employer adds up to 3% of eligible pay when you contribute 6%. Matching can be calculated each paycheck. A year-end true-up may reconcile differences, but only if the plan provides one. Confirm the rules before changing contribution timing.

What is a target-date fund?

A fund holding a mix of investments that generally changes over time toward and sometimes beyond a target retirement year. Funds with the same year can have different risks and fees. They are not guaranteed. Mixing one with other funds changes your combined allocation; this tool does not look through to the underlying holdings.

Am I on track for retirement?

This snapshot cannot answer that on its own. Retirement readiness depends on future spending, retirement age, other savings, Social Security, taxes and uncertain returns. Use this tool to understand your plan first, then build a broader retirement plan. A high balance, full match or low fee alone does not establish readiness.

Leaving this job: what happens to my 401(k)?

You can usually leave it in the plan, move it to a new employer’s plan, roll it to an IRA, or cash it out. Asking for a direct rollover avoids the 20% withholding on a payout. See what to do with your old 401(k) for the options, the rules and a cash-out calculator.

How the estimates work

Contribution illustrations use eligible pay × contribution rate. Match tiers apply sequentially to the entered rate. Annual fund-cost estimates use each current balance × expense ratio, before market changes. Fee estimates exclude unknown amounts, transaction/service charges and costs not entered. No tax limits or investment returns are modeled, and no fund is recommended.
