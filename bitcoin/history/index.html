2008

The idea

  1. 31 October 2008Origins
    01 / ORIGINS↗

    Satoshi publishes the Bitcoin white paper

    A nine-page proposal introduces peer-to-peer electronic cash.

    Explore this momentClose details+
    9pages. A different starting point.

    Someone using the name Satoshi Nakamoto shares a nine-page paper with a cryptography mailing list. It describes how people could send electronic payments directly to one another, using a shared record and proof of work instead of a central operator.

    Why it mattered

    Bitcoin brought earlier cryptographic ideas together into a design for preventing the same digital money from being spent twice without a trusted intermediary.

    Keep it in perspective

    This was a proposal, not yet a running network. Satoshi’s real-world identity remains unproven.

2009

A network begins

  1. 3 January 2009Origins
    02 / ORIGINS↗

    The genesis block starts the chain

    Block zero anchors the chain that follows.

    Explore this momentClose details+

    Satoshi creates block zero, known as the genesis block. Embedded inside is a headline from The Times about a second bank bailout. The software becomes publicly available later that month, allowing others to join.

    Why it mattered

    The idea now has a starting record. Every later Bitcoin block ultimately connects back to this one.

    Keep it in perspective

    The genesis block’s 50 BTC output cannot be spent. The newspaper headline is real; interpretations of Satoshi’s full intentions are still interpretations.

2012

Rules in action

  1. 1 April 2012Protocol
    12 / PROTOCOL↗

    P2SH makes complex wallets more practical

    Pay-to-script-hash supports simpler payment addresses for more complex spending conditions.

    Explore this momentClose details+

    BIP 16 introduces pay-to-script-hash validation rules. A sender can pay a compact address while the recipient supplies the spending script when the funds are later spent.

    Why it mattered

    Arrangements such as multisignature wallets become easier to fund without making the sender handle all the underlying conditions.

    Keep it in perspective

    P2SH is a soft fork: a tighter set of validation rules. It creates neither a separate coin nor an investment return.

  2. 28 November 2012 · UTCMonetary policy
    13 / MONETARY POLICY◈

    The first halving

    The block subsidy falls from 50 to 25 BTC.

    Explore this momentClose details+
    50 → 25BTC subsidy per block.

    At block 210,000, the subsidy for each new block falls from 50 BTC to 25 BTC. This is the first halving: a scheduled reduction in how quickly new bitcoin enters circulation.

    Why it mattered

    The issuance schedule is enforced by the network’s rules. It does not depend on a committee deciding how much new money to create.

    Keep it in perspective

    The subsidy halves every 210,000 blocks, roughly every four years. Transaction fees are separate, and a reduction in new supply does not guarantee a price increase.

2017

Who decides the rules?

  1. 12 March 2017Forks & governance
    27 / FORKS & GOVERNANCE⑂

    UASF: users propose an activation deadline

    BIP 148 gives the SegWit debate a 1 August deadline.

    Explore this momentClose details+

    The BIP 148 proposal describes a user-activated soft fork, or UASF. Participating nodes would reject blocks that failed to signal for SegWit during its enforcement window.

    Why it mattered

    It makes the role of economically meaningful users and validating nodes central to the argument about activation.

    Keep it in perspective

    The mechanism comes with a risk of incompatible chains if participants disagree. Later miner signaling through BIP 91 changes the situation before the deadline.

    Explore this momentClose details+

    Signatories to the New York Agreement support SegWit activation and a later increase in the base block-size limit. The combined plan becomes known as SegWit2x.

    Why it mattered

    The agreement tests whether coordination among prominent companies and miners can produce a rule change accepted across the network.

    Keep it in perspective

    The signatories’ own announcement records their commitments. It does not establish unanimous agreement among developers, node operators or users.

    SegWit activation is a sequence of proposals, signaling and enforcement—not a single switch flipped by one group.

ONE WORD. DIFFERENT MEANINGS.

What exactly is a fork?

Following the branches is easier when the distinctions are clear.

01

A soft fork

A compatible tightening of validation rules. P2SH, SegWit and Taproot are examples. There is no new coin just because a soft fork activates.

See SegWit →
02

A chain split

Incompatible rules produce separate ledgers with a shared earlier history. BCH split from BTC; BSV and the branch later called eCash subsequently split within the BCH family.

See the Bitcoin Cash split →
03

A code fork

Software is reused to build a separate network. Namecoin and Litecoin borrowed from Bitcoin’s code without dividing the BTC ledger into two histories.

See the early code forks →
