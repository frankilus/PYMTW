What the book explores

Bitcoin is usually discussed as an asset to buy and hold. Digital Credit takes a different angle: how a truly scarce, bearer digital money reshapes the oldest idea in finance — yield, and the credit relationships that produce it.

  • How "yield" has traditionally been created — and its hidden trade-offs
  • What changes when the underlying money can't be debased
  • Bitcoin and the evolution of credit markets
  • Risk, counterparty trust, and the difference between real and manufactured yield
  • What responsible, long-term holders should understand about "earning" on Bitcoin
A responsible lens. The book is educational and analytical — not a pitch for any yield product, lending platform, or "passive income" scheme. Nothing in it is individualized investment advice, and PYMTW never takes custody of funds or promises returns.
